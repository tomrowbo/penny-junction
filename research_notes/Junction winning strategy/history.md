# Junction flagship history, winners, and winning patterns

## What official tracks/challenges existed each year?

### Takeaway
The public record is strongest at the individual-challenge level, not at a stable year-by-year “track” taxonomy. Junction’s current archive normalizes challenges into ten broad categories (AI & Data, Education, Entertainment, Finance, Health, IoT & Hardware, Other, Security, Sustainability, Urban & Mobility), but some archive year labels appear inconsistent with contemporaneous participant accounts, so the lists below should be treated as a useful sample rather than a definitive complete program.

### Cited Findings
- Junction’s official archive says it contains 147 challenges from nine editions and 118 partner companies and lets users filter by year, track, or partner; its normalized track filters are AI & Data, Education, Entertainment, Finance, Health, IoT & Hardware, Other, Security, Sustainability, and Urban & Mobility. — [Junction official challenge archive](https://www.hackjunction.com/challenges/)
- For 2022, the official archive displays at least these challenges: Aalto University’s “Energy revolution, now”; CGI’s “Now Green Code you must”; Wolt’s “Better cities. Delivered”; Huawei’s “Work Remotely with Huawei”; Keksintösäätiö’s “Paving the way for tomorrow’s inventions”; CGI/KONE/Meyer Turku’s “Cruising with Robots”; McKinsey Digital’s “Unleash Geospatial Analytics”; Nexi’s “Crack the Payments”; and BNB Chain’s “The Road Towards Society 2.0.” — [Junction official challenge archive](https://www.hackjunction.com/challenges/)
- A contemporary event listing independently confirms that Junction 2022 was held November 4–6 in Espoo with 1,300 participants and partner challenges from companies including Huawei, Binance, and Aiven. — [University of Tartu event listing](https://digihum.ut.ee/en/sisu/junction-2022)
- For the 2023 flagship event, participant and partner accounts confirm challenges including Pixieray’s “Peek Behind the Eyes,” Huawei’s “Sense the Motion,” Tietoevry’s “PainMgmt: Safer Solutions,” Aalto University’s “Resource Revolution, now” and “Energy Revolution, now,” and Veikkaus’s “Next Level of Interactive Gaming Experiences.” — [Serpentine AI participant recap](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/serpentineai_junction2023-serpentine-top20-activity-7136345817346396160-YLSd); [Aalto challenge winner’s account](https://pettiboy.com/); [Aalto energy winner account](https://fi.linkedin.com/in/eetu-pakkanen-163881261); [Junction official archive](https://www.hackjunction.com/challenges/)
- The official archive also associates 2023 with BNB Chain’s “Real-time data indexing,” Cronvall’s sustainable industrial purchasing challenge, Smartly.io’s “Produce More Content with Less!,” plus Aiven and Google Cloud side challenges. — [Junction official challenge archive](https://www.hackjunction.com/challenges/)
- Contemporary 2024 participant accounts confirm challenges from HKFoods (reducing food-production giveaway/waste), Fingrid (transparent customer-request and development communication), Sitra (democratic participation with privacy), Lääkärikeskus Aava (“ThriveConnect”), and an equipment-inventory inspection/survey problem that produced the overall winner. — [HKFoods challenge winner recap](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kalisch-daniel_worldskills-junction-junction2023-activity-7262751125701836800-Kndo); [Fingrid winner recap](https://fi.linkedin.com/in/leerichchi); [Sitra winner recap](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/svsofia_this-weekend-we-won-first-place-in-the-junction-activity-7261728789192196096-UorZ); [Aava participant recap](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/pzkraft_junction2024-hackathon-innovation-activity-7263109415149424640-nbeT); [equipment-inventory participant recap](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kelciumyh_my-first-hackathon-experience-was-a-big-one-activity-7263147846932004864-pyNf)
- Junction’s official 2025 platform described the umbrella theme as “Utopia & Dystopia,” positioned AI as a toolkit, named partners including Sensofusion, Supercell, Pfizer, Aalto University and WithSecure, allowed teams of up to five, and advertised a €50,000+ total pool with a €10,000 grand prize. — [Official Junction Platform event page](https://hackjunction.app/hackathons/junction-2025)
- The official archive lists 2025 challenges including Fortum energy forecasting, Harvia “Sauna Intelligence,” P&G marketing, Pfizer/Aava “Head Start,” Sensofusion “Lights, Camera, Reaction!” and “Radio City,” Bank of Finland AI-for-regulation, Figma Make prompt-to-code, n8n agentic AI, Snap’s real-world AI companion, and tech challenges from Google Cloud, SAS, ElevenLabs, Starstuff, and Featherless.ai. — [Junction official challenge archive](https://www.hackjunction.com/challenges/)
- The 2025 event had 18 challenge winners and 11 tech-challenge winners, according to Junction’s own announcement reposted by a participant/employer. — [Junction winner announcement repost](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/zhang-zeyu_looks-like-our-intern-arjun-just-won-junction-activity-7395845983248752641-TaKb)

### Inferences
- Junction has steadily shifted from broad “track” branding toward many concrete sponsor problems plus cross-cutting technology side challenges; for competitors, the sponsor brief and sponsor judging criteria matter more than the archive’s generic category label. — [Junction official challenge archive](https://www.hackjunction.com/challenges/); [Official Junction Platform event page](https://hackjunction.app/hackathons/junction-2025)
- AI became nearly ubiquitous by 2025, but a non-AI project won the AI-themed flagship, suggesting that technical novelty and problem fit beat superficial theme compliance. — [WAVELET repository](https://github.com/ron0studios/WAVELET); [official 2025 event page](https://hackjunction.app/hackathons/junction-2025)

### Gaps
- The current official archive appears to have at least one year-mapping problem: it labels Pixieray/Huawei/Tietoevry/Aalto challenges as 2024, while contemporaneous participant accounts explicitly place those challenges at Junction 2023. Do not use its year labels alone without cross-checking. — [Junction official archive](https://www.hackjunction.com/challenges/); [contemporaneous 2023 participant recap](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/serpentineai_junction2023-serpentine-top20-activity-7136345817346396160-YLSd)
- No complete, authoritative static list of all challenges and every track winner for 2022–2025 was discoverable; older annual microsites have been retired or reworked.

## Which overall and track winners won, what did they build, and which sponsor/problem did each address?

### Takeaway
The four most recent confirmed overall winners solved very different problems—youth mental health, driver-fatigue safety, industrial inventory, and radio-wave simulation—but each had a working, immediately legible demo and a strong “show, don’t tell” hook. Sponsor challenge wins reveal the same pattern: narrow problem framing, clear user value, and enough polish to be understood quickly.

### Cited Findings
- **2022 overall — Bitter Sweet / Gojingamrae, project “Haru.”** Four Korean team members built a teen diary app that uses AI to generate a picture from each day’s entry; it won the €10,000 Junction championship and the Google Cloud side challenge. The team said the concept began from trying to improve teenagers’ mental health. — [Asia Business Daily report](https://view.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2022111311381508794)
- **2022 example track finalist — BCG sustainability.** A team won the “Sustainable Future” track and placed third in the BCG challenge with an app that gamified household energy reduction and acted as a hub for controlling appliances. — [participant account](https://fi.linkedin.com/in/tuukka-miettinen)
- **2022 example partner winner — Smartly.io.** Team Glados won Smartly.io’s challenge with “Mishmash,” described as an infinite digital content-creation tool. — [team member’s award record](https://fi.linkedin.com/in/andreasurbanski)
- **2023 overall — Driving Change, project “In the Blink for Safety.”** Markus Andersson, David Enberg, Alex Granlund and Johannes Peltola used Pixieray eyeglass eye/head-tracking data combined with GPS to detect driver fatigue and let drivers review daily driving performance; a member says it was selected from 240 teams. — [winner Alex Granlund](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/granlunda_junction2023-activity-7129777848134873088-E_A-); [winner Markus Andersson](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/markus-andersson-1a77a1196_junction2023-activity-7129786270473461760-DxXw); [winner David Enberg](https://fi.linkedin.com/in/david-enberg)
- **2023 Pixieray challenge winner (distinct from overall winner) — MelodEye.** Serpentine AI says this project combined eye tracking and heartbeat frames to infer eye movement, heart rate, breathing and emotional state, then respond with personalized playlists; the challenge win put it in the top six overall. — [Serpentine AI recap](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/serpentineai_junction2023-serpentine-top20-activity-7136345817346396160-YLSd)
- **2023 Aalto “Resource Revolution, now” winner — ReMaskIt.** The team built an Instagram filter and app for recycling education with Google ML Kit, React Native, Flask and GPT-4 Vision. — [winner’s portfolio](https://pettiboy.com/)
- **2023 Aalto “Energy Revolution, now” winner.** The team proposed storing excess summer solar electricity rather than exporting it into low/negative-price markets, then using it during high-demand winter periods. — [winner account](https://fi.linkedin.com/in/eetu-pakkanen-163881261)
- **2024 overall — BREAKDANCE, by Frank Sandqvist.** Junction’s official profile says Sandqvist built an AI-driven inventory-management app that gamified equipment reporting, won €10,000, and supported the final presentation with an AI-generated rap video that he believes helped. — [official Junction profile](https://www.hackjunction.com/blog/frank-sandqvist-story)
- **2024 second overall — Sonaptics.** A former overall winner’s team earned €5,000 with a hybrid hardware/software, touchless interface for reporting food-production-line problems. — [team member’s award record](https://fi.linkedin.com/in/andreasurbanski)
- **2024 Sitra challenge winner.** A five-person team built a browser extension that notices when a user is reading about an actionable civic topic and points toward participation platforms; it used on-device Gemini Nano/experimental Chrome APIs so most processing stayed local for privacy. — [winner Sofia Vereshchagina](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/svsofia_this-weekend-we-won-first-place-in-the-junction-activity-7261728789192196096-UorZ)
- **2024 Fingrid challenge winner — Aligned.** The project was a two-way customer dashboard with automated release notifications, request status, prioritization rationale, and feedback; its team says the event had 1,000+ participants and 180+ teams. — [winner Chi Lee](https://fi.linkedin.com/in/leerichchi)
- **2024 HKFoods challenge winner.** A WorldSkills team built a prototype to improve cross-department communication and auto-translate messages around food-production under/overweight waste. Its recap says branding, presentation and UI/UX were the most important lessons carried forward from failing in 2023. — [winner Daniel Kalisch](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kalisch-daniel_worldskills-junction-junction2023-activity-7262751125701836800-Kndo)
- **2025 overall — WAVELET.** Rudrrayan Manna, Arjun Juneja, Keanu Czirjak and Akshith Alluri won both Sensofusion’s “Radio City” challenge and the €10,000 grand prize with a real-time radio/EM simulation of Hong Kong based on Maxwell’s equations, built in pure C++/OpenGL and accelerated with GPU compute shaders; the engine visualized interference, absorption, signal hot spots and dead zones. — [project repository](https://github.com/ron0studios/WAVELET); [Sensofusion’s recap](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/hypponen_we-at-sensofusion-had-an-excellent-time-at-activity-7396199187996045312-QXf6)
- **2025 overall runner-up — LoreDash.** Frank Sandqvist used multiple AI systems to create a minigame for arbitrary Wikipedia articles; it was a Supercell challenge runner-up and second overall for €7,500. — [official Junction profile](https://www.hackjunction.com/blog/frank-sandqvist-story); [Junction video index](https://junctionhackathon.ruclips.net/)
- **2025 Sensofusion camera winner — Academic Menaces.** Sensofusion says the team won its camera-vision challenge with “remarkable technical achievements”; both Sensofusion challenge winners reached the overall top five. — [Sensofusion recap](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/hypponen_we-at-sensofusion-had-an-excellent-time-at-activity-7396199187996045312-QXf6)

### Inferences
- Winners often combine a technically credible core with a human problem a judge can grasp in one sentence: teen mental health, drowsy driving, tedious inventory reporting, or invisible radio coverage. — [2022 report](https://view.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2022111311381508794); [2023 winner account](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/markus-andersson-1a77a1196_junction2023-activity-7129786270473461760-DxXw); [2024 official profile](https://www.hackjunction.com/blog/frank-sandqvist-story); [2025 repository](https://github.com/ron0studios/WAVELET)
- The strongest demonstrations make an invisible process visible or interactive: generated diary art, fatigue traces, gamified inventory, and live EM waves. — [2022 report](https://view.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2022111311381508794); [2023 winner account](https://fi.linkedin.com/in/david-enberg); [2024 official profile](https://www.hackjunction.com/blog/frank-sandqvist-story); [2025 repository](https://github.com/ron0studios/WAVELET)

### Gaps
- Public results do not preserve every challenge winner, runner-up, project page, judging score, or sponsor rubric from 2022–2025.
- Some 2023 sources casually congratulated JunctionX qualifiers as a “winning team” even though their own later recap says they did not win the Finland flagship; those references were excluded from the winner dataset. — [JunctionX Spain team account](https://es.linkedin.com/in/samuel-mula-7617981ab)

## What recurring technical, product, demo, and team traits appear among winners?

### Takeaway
The evidence points to a repeatable playbook: choose a painful and specific sponsor problem, validate the interpretation with mentors, build one visibly working vertical slice, give it a memorable identity, and spend real time on the pitch. Deep technology can win, but only when the audience can immediately see why it matters.

### Cited Findings
- Junction’s official 2025 rules say challenge-track winners are chosen by the companies providing the challenges, while the overall winner is selected through peer review followed by live pitches from the top projects. — [official Junction Platform event page](https://hackjunction.app/hackathons/junction-2025)
- Junction’s 2025 retrospective explicitly encouraged hackers to brainstorm with partner representatives; sponsors said they wanted insights turned into action, new approaches to important issues, and something unpredictable or shocking. — [official 2025 retrospective](https://www.hackjunction.com/blog/48-hours-junction-2025)
- Junction’s official team advice emphasizes diverse skills, adaptability, learning fast, and complementary specialists rather than competing generalists. — [official Junction 2023 team-building advice](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/hackjunction_junction-hackathon-hackathons-activity-7122647547260657664-MUU-)
- Multi-time winner Frank Sandqvist’s advice is to identify strengths, assemble a team around complementary strengths, build something the team genuinely cares about, make the final product clean and realistic, and present it in a fun, attention-catching way. — [official Junction profile](https://www.hackjunction.com/blog/frank-sandqvist-story)
- The 2024 BREAKDANCE win paired an understandable operational tool with gamification and a memorable AI-rap presentation; the creator himself thinks the video may have boosted the result. — [official Junction profile](https://www.hackjunction.com/blog/frank-sandqvist-story)
- A 2024 participant who tackled the same inventory brief concluded that a deep but unfinished solution loses to a fast effective prototype, and that visuals and clear explanations matter more than niche jargon. — [participant retrospective](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kelciumyh_my-first-hackathon-experience-was-a-big-one-activity-7263147846932004864-pyNf)
- The 2024 HKFoods challenge winners likewise credited branding, solution presentation, and UI/UX as lessons that turned their prior-year failure into a challenge win; they also stress staying focused on the challenge’s actual problems. — [winner retrospective](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kalisch-daniel_worldskills-junction-junction2023-activity-7262751125701836800-Kndo)
- The Sitra-winning team combined a nearly frictionless front-end concept with substantial on-device engineering; one designer says judges praised its front-end simplicity and “cool” back-end implementation. — [winner Claudio Lintunen](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/claudiolintunen_junction2024-junctionhackathon-sitrachallenge-activity-7261752253017939969-Aj_x); [technical teammate Sofia Vereshchagina](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/svsofia_this-weekend-we-won-first-place-in-the-junction-activity-7261728789192196096-UorZ)
- WAVELET won despite using no AI at the “biggest AI hackathon,” indicating that from-scratch technical ambition, real-time visual impact, and exact sponsor relevance can outperform a generic AI wrapper. — [WAVELET repository](https://github.com/ron0studios/WAVELET); [team member account](https://uk.linkedin.com/in/akshith-alluri-b24863268)
- The overall winners from 2022–2025 all delivered demonstrable products rather than decks alone: a diary-to-image app, a fatigue detector using real sensor/GPS data, a gamified inventory app, and a live GPU simulation. — [2022 report](https://view.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2022111311381508794); [2023 winner account](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/markus-andersson-1a77a1196_junction2023-activity-7129786270473461760-DxXw); [2024 official profile](https://www.hackjunction.com/blog/frank-sandqvist-story); [2025 repository](https://github.com/ron0studios/WAVELET)

### Inferences
- **Technical:** Prefer a risky-looking but bounded technical feat with a reliable live path; keep a recorded fallback. The winning technical work is distinctive (sensor fusion, on-device inference, C++/GPU physics), not a broad pile of integrations. — [2023 winner](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/markus-andersson-1a77a1196_junction2023-activity-7129786270473461760-DxXw); [2024 Sitra winner](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/svsofia_this-weekend-we-won-first-place-in-the-junction-activity-7261728789192196096-UorZ); [2025 repository](https://github.com/ron0studios/WAVELET)
- **Product:** Phrase the project as “specific user + costly moment + observable outcome.” A small polished workflow that answers the sponsor brief is better than a sprawling platform. — [HKFoods winner retrospective](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kalisch-daniel_worldskills-junction-junction2023-activity-7262751125701836800-Kndo); [inventory participant retrospective](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kelciumyh_my-first-hackathon-experience-was-a-big-one-activity-7263147846932004864-pyNf)
- **Demo:** The first 20–30 seconds should show the before/after visually, and the pitch needs a name, story, clean interface and memorable moment. BREAKDANCE’s rap and WAVELET’s mesmerizing field visualization are strong examples. — [official Junction profile](https://www.hackjunction.com/blog/frank-sandqvist-story); [WAVELET repository](https://github.com/ron0studios/WAVELET)
- **Team:** Two brothers can compete effectively if roles are sharply divided—one owns the working core and reliability, the other owns user flow, visual polish, partner conversations and pitch—with both converging for integration and rehearsal. This is an inference from Junction’s complementary-team guidance rather than a directly measured winning-team rule. — [official Junction team-building advice](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/hackjunction_junction-hackathon-hackathons-activity-7122647547260657664-MUU-)
- **Tactics:** Speak to sponsor mentors early with a one-sentence interpretation of the problem, show a crude demo midway, and ask what would disqualify the project. Partner judges choose challenge winners, so correcting alignment early has unusually high leverage. — [official rules](https://hackjunction.app/hackathons/junction-2025); [official retrospective](https://www.hackjunction.com/blog/48-hours-junction-2025)

### Gaps
- Junction does not publish comparable judging scorecards across years, so these patterns are qualitative and should not be treated as causal statistical findings.
- Public evidence overrepresents winners who posted detailed retrospectives; silent teams and failed projects are not available as a balanced control group.

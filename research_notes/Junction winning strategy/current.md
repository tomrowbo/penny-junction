## What do sponsors and the grand jury appear to want?

### Takeaway
Official judge statements converge sharply: real user problem, unique insight, working return-worthy product, robustness/evaluation, and a path to production/scale. “AI because AI” or a polished but generic feature is explicitly disfavored.

### Cited Findings
- Grand-jury member Kia Kanninen says she wants a problem worth solving, not merely an easy-to-build feature. — [Official judge profiles](https://2026.hackjunction.com/#judges)
- Daksh Raghuvanshi wants agent teams to explain exactly where their agent fails and to use a reward/evaluation that survives adversarial pressure. — [Official judge profiles](https://2026.hackjunction.com/#judges)
- Otto Söderlund wants a real user problem and something people would return to; Hubertus van der Drift wants speed, pragmatism, and one unique insight that makes the solution 10× better/faster. — [Official judge profiles](https://2026.hackjunction.com/#judges)
- Verda CTO Arturs Polis wants hard problems plus prototypes with a clear route to production and scale. — [Official judge profiles](https://2026.hackjunction.com/#judges)
- Hostinger’s keynote premise is that AI has commoditized building; what is scarce is attention, timing, and real problems, with emphasis on validating demand before building. — [Official Junction schedule](https://2026.hackjunction.com/#schedule)
- Partner-track winners are selected by each Challenge Partner’s own criteria; the overall winner passes face-to-face peer review into a top five, then a live pitch and grand-jury decision. — [Official judging description](https://2026.hackjunction.com/#judging)

### Inferences
- Winning checklist inferred directly from the jury: name one painful user/job; state one non-obvious insight; demonstrate the critical loop live; show one credible metric/evaluation and one failure case; explain why the sponsor’s data/hardware/API is essential; end with the production/scale path.
- Peer review means the project must be understandable and memorable to fellow hackers, not only sponsor experts. A one-sentence pitch and a fast visual payoff are strategically important.
- For an agent, a tiny evaluation harness or “red team” view is likely more differentiating than adding another model/tool integration.

### Gaps
- No universal numeric scoring rubric or weights were publicly posted; partner criteria can differ.

### Inferences
- Submit a minimal but coherent preliminary entry before doing deep implementation; the five-hour window from hacking start to preliminary deadline is too short to leave naming, team, track, and description until the end.
- Decide the primary partner challenge at opening, then add at most one compatible tech bounty. Chasing many sponsor integrations risks a generic, brittle demo.
- Freeze features Saturday night, reserve Sunday morning for the submission page, repository/license/readme, demo video if required, and a rehearsed sub-two-minute live story.

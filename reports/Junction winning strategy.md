# Win Junction by proving one insight

**Bottom line:** your best route is not “build the most”; it is to choose **one concrete partner problem where you and your brother have an unfair execution advantage**, validate your interpretation with that partner tonight, and make one valuable transformation impossible to miss. Junction 2026 has six public tracks—Hardware, Healthcare, AI Infrastructure, FinTech, Agentic AI and Data—and a large partner roster, but many complete briefs and judging details remain inside the participant Platform or opening material. The last four overall winners ranged from an AI diary-art app to a C++/GPU radio simulator, so the repeatable pattern is not a technology: it is **a narrow human problem, a distinctive working core, an immediately legible visual demo and a memorable presentation**. For a two-person team, split ownership: one brother protects the technical proof and demo reliability; the other protects problem fit, sponsor contact, interface, submission and pitch. Optimize first for the partner award, then package the same work for peer review and the grand jury; do not build two projects. Treat all event-day details below as checked on **13 November 2026**, but verify them against the Platform, opening briefing and Discord because announcements were still appearing immediately before the event. ([Junction 2026](https://2026.hackjunction.com/); [event Platform](https://hackjunction.app/hackathons/junction-2026))

## The first 90 minutes determine the weekend

Do not start coding when hacking opens at 19:30 EET. During the first 20 minutes, each of you should independently shortlist no more than three challenges from the complete Platform briefs, then score them together using the table below. During minutes 20–50, split up and speak to the relevant partner desks. Return with exact answers to the sponsor questions below, especially the disqualifiers and what a winning demo must prove. During minutes 50–70, select one primary challenge and at most one compatible tech bounty, write the one-sentence thesis, and sketch the 90-second demo backwards from its final visual payoff. During minutes 70–90, test the hardest technical assumption with the ugliest possible spike. If it fails, switch challenges now, not Saturday afternoon.

Write this before building: **“For [specific user], [painful moment] causes [observable cost]. We improve [one outcome] by [credible factor or clear before/after] because [non-obvious insight].”** Then define a single golden-path interaction that proves it. Junction’s partner criteria commonly cover relevance, novelty, technical quality, user experience, completeness/demo-ability, and feasibility or impact; judges can actually run submitted software, call endpoints and use provided credentials. ([Junction terms](https://www.hackjunction.com/terms-of-service)) The 2026 grand jury reinforces the same priorities: a problem worth solving, repeat use, one 10× insight, explicit evaluation and failure modes for agents, and a path from prototype to production. ([2026 judges](https://2026.hackjunction.com/#judges))

### Use this track-selection scorecard

Score each candidate 1–5 after—not before—speaking with its partner. Double the “proof by Sunday” score. The highest total is your default choice unless it depends on unavailable hardware, data or expertise.

| Criterion | Weight | What a 5 looks like |
|---|---:|---|
| Proof by Sunday | **2×** | A live before/after with a number, visual or physical result |
| Your unfair advantage | 1× | You already know the domain, stack, hardware or algorithm |
| Pain and user clarity | 1× | One named user and an expensive or frequent moment |
| Unique insight | 1× | A defensible idea beyond “add an LLM” |
| Data/hardware access | 1× | Inputs work tonight; no promised access dependency |
| Sponsor-rubric clarity | 1× | Partner can state the winning proof and disqualifiers |
| Mentor responsiveness | 1× | You can get feedback at least twice on Saturday |
| Visual/memorable demo | 1× | A peer grasps the payoff in 20 seconds |
| Production credibility | 1× | Clear next user, deployment and scaling step |
| Competition opportunity | 1× | Fewer credible entrants, if organizers will disclose this |

Ask every shortlisted sponsor: **Who is the exact user and when does this hurt? What is the minimum acceptable use of your data/API/hardware? What would instantly disqualify a project? What separates a good entry from the winner? What metric or scenario should the demo prove? May we use synthetic data? Will judges run the app, and on what device/network? Can we show you a rough prototype tomorrow? How many teams appear to be entering?** Ask organizers separately for the peer-review question, pitch duration, mandatory video format, pre-existing-code policy, AI disclosure rule and exact final form fields; these were not all public at research time.

The Friday preliminary submission is due at **23:55 on the Platform** (the marketing schedule rounds it to 23:59), only a few hours after hacking begins. Submit a coherent name, problem, challenge and intended proof early. The final submission is due **Sunday at 10:00**, with review from 10:30. ([Platform schedule](https://hackjunction.app/hackathons/junction-2026); [official schedule](https://2026.hackjunction.com/#schedule))

## Four winners reveal one durable pattern

Junction’s archive is useful but not fully reliable: it appears to label some Pixieray, Huawei, Tietoevry and Aalto challenges as 2024 even though contemporaneous participants place them in 2023. Use it for discovery, not as the sole source of year attribution. ([challenge archive](https://www.hackjunction.com/challenges/); [contemporaneous 2023 account](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/serpentineai_junction2023-serpentine-top20-activity-7136345817346396160-YLSd)) The table below therefore restricts itself to cross-checked overall winners.

| Year | Overall winner | What made the project legible |
|---|---|---|
| **2022** | **Bitter Sweet / “Haru”**—a teen diary app that generated artwork from daily entries; it also won the Google Cloud side challenge. ([report](https://view.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2022111311381508794)) | Emotional problem, one-step transformation, visible personal result. |
| **2023** | **Driving Change / “In the Blink for Safety”**—combined Pixieray eye/head tracking with GPS to detect driver fatigue and review driving performance, selected from a reported 240 teams. ([winner account](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/granlunda_junction2023-activity-7129777848134873088-E_A-); [technical account](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/markus-andersson-1a77a1196_junction2023-activity-7129786270473461760-DxXw)) | Real sensor data, safety stakes, technically distinctive fusion and a clear dashboard. |
| **2024** | **Frank Sandqvist / “BREAKDANCE”**—an AI-driven inventory app that gamified equipment reporting, supported by a memorable AI-rap presentation. ([official profile](https://www.hackjunction.com/blog/frank-sandqvist-story)) | Mundane industrial pain made engaging; clean product plus a recall device. |
| **2025** | **WAVELET**—a real-time Hong Kong radio/EM simulator based on Maxwell’s equations, written in C++/OpenGL and GPU compute shaders; it also won Sensofusion’s “Radio City.” ([repository](https://github.com/ron0studios/WAVELET); [Sensofusion recap](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/hypponen_we-at-sensofusion-had-an-excellent-time-at-activity-7396199187996045312-QXf6)) | Deep sponsor fit, hard technical core and an arresting visualization of invisible physics—without AI. |

Across these very different winners, four traits recur. First, each can be explained as **specific user/problem → visible transformation** in one breath. Second, the technically impressive part is bounded: sensor fusion, a focused workflow, or one simulation engine—not a catalogue of integrations. Third, each has an interface or visual moment peers can remember. Fourth, the partner-specific solution also tells a universal story, which matters because challenge partners choose their winners, then face-to-face peer review narrows the overall field before a live grand-jury pitch. ([2026 judging](https://2026.hackjunction.com/#judging))

Challenge-winner evidence points the same way. A 2024 HKFoods team credited **branding, presentation and UI/UX**, plus focus on the actual problem, for turning a prior failure into a win. ([winner retrospective](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kalisch-daniel_worldskills-junction-junction2023-activity-7262751125701836800-Kndo)) A Sitra-winning team paired a frictionless browser-extension idea with substantive on-device engineering. ([technical account](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/svsofia_this-weekend-we-won-first-place-in-the-junction-activity-7261728789192196096-UorZ)) WAVELET then showed that a non-AI entry could win an AI-heavy edition through exact relevance, from-scratch ambition and visual impact. The useful conclusion is not “build hardware” or “avoid AI”; it is **make the difficult thing visible and make every difficult thing serve the problem**.

## Two people can win by protecting the demo

Assign **Brother A: product, sponsor and story owner**. He maintains the rubric checklist, interviews partners and prospective users, owns the interface and golden-path flow, writes the submission, records the fallback video and leads the pitch. Assign **Brother B: technical proof and reliability owner**. He attacks the hardest assumption first, owns data/model/backend/hardware, deployment, instrumentation, evaluation, README and demo recovery. Pair on the initial architecture, the first end-to-end slice, and every rehearsal. Both must be able to give the 60-second explanation and restart the demo.

| Window | Required outcome—not activity |
|---|---|
| **Friday 19:30–21:00** | Partner-validated challenge choice, one-sentence thesis, scored alternatives, kill-risk spike and 90-second demo storyboard. |
| **Friday 21:00–23:40** | Deployed thin vertical slice, even if ugly; preliminary submission completed before the 23:55 deadline. Attend only a workshop that removes your biggest risk. |
| **Overnight / early Saturday** | Stable data path and first real golden-path result. Stagger rest so both of you are not impaired simultaneously; the venue has no dedicated sleeping area. ([FAQ](https://2026.hackjunction.com/#faq)) |
| **Saturday morning** | First usable demo; five small test cases; sponsor review of problem interpretation. Cut features that do not serve the rubric. |
| **Saturday afternoon** | Two- to three-hour build → test → mentor loops; one metric, baseline or evaluation; screenshots and evidence captured as you go. |
| **Saturday evening** | **Feature freeze.** Clean-device test, synthetic/sanitized data, restricted credentials, graceful failures, README/setup, fallback recording and draft submission complete. |
| **Sunday before 09:30** | Two timed rehearsals, bad-network/expired-key/empty-state test, every link checked, submit early. No architectural changes. |
| **After submission** | Rehearse a 60-second peer-review pitch and the longer jury version; prepare concise technical, user, safety and scaling answers. |

Use this **100-point project rubric** at every feature decision: problem pain and sponsor relevance 20; working technical proof 20; unique insight 15; visible user outcome 15; evaluation/evidence 10; polish and reliability 10; memorable story 5; production path 5. Any task that cannot raise one of these scores within two hours is a candidate for deletion.

Your pitch should run: **10 seconds** for the user and costly moment; **10 seconds** for the surprising insight; **45–60 seconds** for the live transformation; **15 seconds** for evidence, including a failure case if AI/agentic; **15 seconds** for why the sponsor asset is essential and what goes into production next; then one memorable closing line or visual. Frank Sandqvist’s advice is to build around team strengths, care about the project, make the product look clean and realistic, and present it in an interesting, fun way. ([official winner profile](https://www.hackjunction.com/blog/frank-sandqvist-story)) A playful ending helps only after the proof works.

For any agent or retrieval product, show more than an answer: expose the source or drawing region, confidence, latency/cost, at least one known failure and a tiny adversarial evaluation set. This directly answers the 2026 jury’s stated interest in where agents fail and whether their reward/evaluation survives pressure. For hardware, keep a recorded proof and a software simulation or replay path, but state clearly what is live and what is simulated. Candor with a credible next step beats a brittle bluff.

The failure modes to police are: solving an easy feature rather than an important problem; a generic AI wrapper with no non-obvious insight; no baseline or evaluation; opaque answers; discovering sponsor misalignment at judging; two disconnected halves that integrate late; coding beyond demo stability; real customer data or overpowered demo credentials; a pitch first written Sunday; and both teammates losing sleep at the same time. The submission is MIT-licensed while your team retains ownership, so avoid secrets and confirm what belongs in the repository. ([Junction FAQ](https://2026.hackjunction.com/#faq))

## Conclusion

Junction’s recent winners do not reward one fashionable stack. They reward **compression**: a complex sponsor problem compressed into one human sentence, a difficult technical claim compressed into a working visual proof, and 48 hours compressed into a story the room remembers. A two-person team has less feature capacity than a four-person team, but that constraint can force the focus that past winners repeatedly display.

The optimal hedge is one project with two narratives: the sponsor hears exact brief compliance and credible continuation; peers and the grand jury see an instantly understandable transformation with one surprising insight. If a feature does not improve either narrative or make the live proof safer, cut it.

# Banks constrain AI around customer money

Other banks do let assistants work across rich customer data, but the visible pattern is **authenticated access through constrained bank capabilities—not a language model with unrestricted SQL**. US incumbents generally keep the conversation tightly controlled: Bank of America says Erica selects predefined answers rather than using generative AI, while Capital One computes Eno insights through event pipelines and specific business logic. European challengers go further: bunq Finn is the closest existing analogue to Penny because it answers questions over bunq and connected external-bank transactions, while Revolut AIR can analyse personal finances and initiate bounded tasks. Their privacy model is not “no personal data reaches AI”; it is consent, minimisation, controlled providers, regional processing, retention limits and no provider training. For Penny, the strongest design is therefore **full authorised data access inside S-Bank, deterministic bank-side financial calculations, and a generative model that receives only the minimum facts needed to explain the answer**.

## Four products reveal three implementation patterns

| Product | What it can access and do | AI/data pattern publicly confirmed | What Penny should learn |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bank of America Erica** | Searches transactions, explains spending, retrieves balances and supports defined banking actions | Bank of America explicitly says Erica uses NLP/ML to select from predefined responses and **does not use generative AI or LLMs** ([Erica](https://info.bankofamerica.com/en/digital-banking/erica)) | Maximum control and predictability, but less conversational freedom |
| **Capital One Eno** | Monitors card transactions and flags duplicate charges, subscription increases, unusual tips and fraud signals | Published architecture uses event-driven AWS services, filters, transformations and insight-specific business logic; public Eno material does not claim an LLM layer ([Capital One architecture](https://www.capitalone.com/tech/cloud/eno-real-time-alerts-powered-by-serverless/)) | Calculate insights before conversation; do not ask an LLM to discover them from raw rows |
| **bunq Finn** | Answers detailed spending questions, supports follow-ups and includes connected external-bank accounts | Creates a temporary request snapshot, retrieves necessary data, and uses Azure OpenAI, Google Gemini and AWS Anthropic APIs in a controlled EU environment; bunq says partners do not permanently retain the data or train on it ([Finn product](https://help.bunq.com/en/articles/meet-finn-your-personal-assistant), [Finn privacy](https://help.bunq.com/articles/how-does-finn-safeguard-my-privacy)) | **Closest competitor to Penny**; multi-bank chat alone is not enough differentiation |
| **Revolut AIR** | Uses transactions, cards and investments for spending breakdowns and can initiate bounded tasks | Revolut explicitly obtains consent to disclose necessary information to technology partners and says those partners operate with zero retention and no external-model training ([AIR launch](https://www.revolut.com/news/revolut_enters_new_era_of_money_intelligence_with_launch_of_ai_assistant/), [legal notice](https://www.revolut.com/legal/rita-disclaimer/)) | Be transparent that controlled processors can see minimised data; constrain actions and provide human escalation |
| **NatWest Spending Insights** | Converses over transactions and produces personalised insights and graphs | NatWest says its 2026 spending product uses proprietary small language models; precise hosting and raw-data flow are not publicly disclosed ([NatWest announcement](https://www.natwestgroup.com/news-and-insights/news-room/press-releases/2026/natwest-group-first-to-unveil-next-generation-of-ai-tools-to-hel.html)) | A bank-controlled model is the clearest route when no customer-derived data may leave the bank environment |

These disclosures support a consistent interpretation: **“access to all data” means all data that the authenticated customer has authorised and that a supported capability requires**. It does not mean every record is placed in the model prompt. None of the reviewed banks publishes evidence that its customer-facing model can issue unrestricted SQL. Their public products expose searches, calculations, insights and actions with defined boundaries; the internal query implementation is generally undisclosed.

## The database stays behind a deterministic boundary

Penny should have complete, customer-scoped analytical reach, including S-Bank history and PSD2-connected accounts, but the model should invoke typed tools such as `spend_by_category`, `cashflow_summary`, `safe_to_spend` and `simulate_reduction`. Bank code should own customer identity, SQL, basket classification and arithmetic. This matches the control philosophy visible in Eno and Erica while retaining the conversational quality of Finn.

If text-to-SQL is ever added, the model still cannot be the security boundary. OWASP recommends enforcing authorisation and privilege separation outside the LLM, validating tool parameters and minimising database permissions ([OWASP guidance](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/LLM_Prompt_Injection_Prevention_Cheat_Sheet.html)). Reference architectures similarly separate generation from execution, validate allowed SQL and use read-only roles ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/database/connect-amazon-bedrock-agents-with-amazon-aurora-postgresql-using-amazon-rds-data-api/), [Google Cloud](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/data-agents/querydata)). For Penny, allow-listed tools are simpler and safer than arbitrary SQL; if free-form analytical SQL becomes necessary, S-Bank should inject the customer scope server-side and add row-level security, a read-only role, single-statement `SELECT` validation, row/time limits and an audit record.

The important product split is: **the financial engine decides; the language model explains**. Safe-to-spend, safe-to-save, cash-flow forecasts and what-if values should be versioned deterministic functions with visible coverage, assumptions and confidence. The model can turn those facts into empathetic language, but it should never invent arithmetic or silently fill missing data. That design directly addresses the EBA’s concerns around hallucination, explainability, data governance, third-party dependence and human oversight in banking AI ([EBA](https://www.eba.europa.eu/publications-and-media/publications/special-topic-artificial-intelligence)).

## “No PII” is the wrong privacy claim

Banks using external model providers do not necessarily keep every customer-derived value inside physical bank infrastructure. bunq publicly names Azure OpenAI, Gemini and AWS Anthropic; Revolut explicitly describes necessary disclosure to technology partners. Their controls are **purpose limitation, minimisation, consent, controlled infrastructure, retention limits and prohibitions on provider training**, not a blanket assertion that no personal information is processed externally.

Removing names, IBANs and raw rows is valuable, but a result such as “your safe-to-spend amount is €437” still relates to an identifiable logged-in customer. The EDPB confirms that pseudonymised data remains personal data when it can be attributed using additional information ([EDPB pseudonymisation guidance](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2025-01/edpb_guidelines_202501_pseudonymisation_en.pdf)). Penny should therefore say:

> Raw transaction records and direct identifiers remain in the bank analytics layer. The language model receives only the minimum customer-derived facts required for this explanation, under controlled retention and no-training terms.

If S-Bank requires **zero customer-derived personal data outside its controlled environment**, minimisation is insufficient; inference must use an approved bank-hosted or tightly governed private deployment. NatWest’s proprietary-model direction is the best public precedent, although NatWest does not disclose enough detail to claim its system is fully on-premises.

## Penny wins through pedagogy, not multi-bank novelty

bunq already combines open banking with conversational transaction search, so Penny should not pitch “chat with all your banks” as the innovation. Its sharper differentiation is **an explainable personal economic trainer built around S-Bank’s three baskets**: mandatory regular, mandatory irregular and everyday spending. Every answer should show which basket drove it, the date range and connected accounts used, the assumption behind the result, and one achievable action. A customer should be able to correct a classification and immediately see the recommendation change.

For the demo, Penny should answer one factual query, one safe-to-spend question and one what-if scenario. The factual query proves breadth across S-Bank and a connected bank. Safe-to-spend proves deterministic reasoning with protected upcoming commitments. The what-if scenario proves pedagogy by showing, for example, how reducing everyday dining by 20% changes monthly savings without shaming the user. The audit trail should record the authenticated session, tool and version, bounded parameters, coverage window, deterministic result, model/version and response—without logging unnecessary raw financial content.

The judge-ready positioning is:

> **Penny combines the breadth of bunq’s multi-bank conversation with the control of traditional banking assistants: S-Bank computes every financial fact inside a customer-scoped analytics layer, and AI turns only approved, minimised results into clear coaching across the three spending baskets.**

## Conclusion

The market validates the product direction, but it also raises the bar. Conversational access to transactions and external accounts already exists; Penny’s defensible advantage is trustworthy training rather than generic chat. Deterministic calculations, explainable assumptions, editable classifications and narrowly scoped data access make the product both more useful to customers and easier for S-Bank to govern.

For the hackathon, the right claim is not that the model can query everything or that no personal data ever reaches a provider. It is that **Penny can use the customer’s complete authorised financial picture without exposing raw banking records or surrendering decision-making to the model**.
